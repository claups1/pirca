Pirca helps small shop owners in Piura, Peru, prepare their business for El Niño costero with a short form and a plan that fits the money they have.

Then open http://localhost:8020. Python 3.12 or newer (tested with 3.14). The six example plans (code PIU-CAST26 and five more) open without any API key and cost 0 tokens. New plans need PORTKEY_API_KEY in a .env file in the project folder or one folder above it.

Why the name Pirca

A pirca is a wall of stones stacked without mortar. The Real Academia Española defines it as "Pared de piedra en seco" (dry-stone wall), from the Quechua word for wall (pirqa; the RAE writes it pirca, 'pared'), used in Argentina, Chile, Ecuador and Peru [1]. In Peru pircas are still built today, for example as dry-stone retaining walls that hold up homes on Lima's hillsides [2].

The app works the same way: the plan is built stone by stone. Each agent adds a stone (the risk, the numbers, the stress test, the final plan) and the code checks make sure every stone holds, so a small shop ends up with a wall between it and the next flood. The live agent map draws that wall as the run progresses (right).

[1] RAE, Diccionario de la lengua española, Edición del Tricentenario, "pirca": dle.rae.es/pirca (entry: "Del quechua pirca 'pared'. 1. f. Arg., Chile, Ec. y Perú. Pared de piedra en seco."). [2] Ita, Santa-Cruz, Daudon and Tarque (2024), "Numerical and experimental pseudo-static study on block arrangement effects in traditional dry-stone walls…", PUCP: cris.pucp.edu.pe.

2Motivation: who has the problem and what it costs today

Who. Owners of small shops in Piura (bodegas, boticas, ferreterías) who sell from a ground floor, keep stock near the floor, depend on suppliers in other cities and have little cash. Pirca's data covers 15 Piura districts; 11 of them are rated high flood hazard and 4 medium (data/districts.csv).

  • Money. In 2017, 59,335 people in Piura reported an economic-loss impact, the highest of any region [HIST-2017-17]. In 2023 the Chamber of Commerce estimated S/ 12–18 million lost from closed premises, out of about S/ 60 million of commerce a day [HIST-2023-32], and moving basic goods became 13% more expensive [HIST-2023-33].
  • Stock and shops. In 2017 water reached 1.20 m inside Castilla's market, three blocks from the river, with shortages and price rises at the Mercado Modelo [HIST-2017-30]. On 24 Mar 2023 a canal overflow flooded the Sullana city market and swept away stalls; traders stopped selling [HIST-2023-34].
  • Time and supply. In 1998 the Panamericana between Tumbes and Piura was cut for three months [HIST-1998-06] and 9 bridges were named as collapsed, including Bolognesi and San Miguel de Piura [HIST-1998-08]. In 2017, 15 bridges were destroyed and 71 affected [HIST-2017-21]; in 2023, 61 vehicle bridges were destroyed [HIST-2023-11], the Bayóvar–Chiclayo road was closed for at least 121 days [HIST-2023-29] and the Paita–Sullana road for 12 days [HIST-2023-30].
  • People. The January–May 2023 rains left 26,605 damnificados and 285,638 afectados in Piura department [HIST-2023-08, HIST-2023-09]. Dengue followed both floods: 41,078 cases through week 25 of 2017 [HIST-2017-25] and 79,643 in 2023 [HIST-2023-36].
  • Frustration. Owners get the same general advice as households, while the questions they face are about their own shop: how much stock to lift, how much cash to keep, which supplier roads will close, and what they can afford to buy this month.

The current alert

ENFEN Comunicado Oficial N° 18-2026 (16 Oct 2026): "Alerta de El Niño Costero". ENFEN says the coastal warming (Niño 1+2) will most likely stay at an extraordinary magnitude until January 2027 and then ease to strong until the start of the southern autumn (March–June) of 2027, with river flows above normal from November in the Tumbes, Chira, Piura, La Leche and Jequetepeque rivers [ENFEN-2026-18]. The earlier communiqué N° 15-2026 gave a ≥62% probability of an extraordinary magnitude for September 2026–January 2027 in Niño 1+2 [ENFEN-2026-15]. The next communiqué is due 30 Oct 2026.

Sources: data/historical_impacts.csv (row IDs in brackets; each row has its link and a quote from the source), data/enfen/index.json and output/sources.md. Of the 99 historical rows, 80 are verified against an official or primary document saved locally and 19 are sourced from press or the Chamber of Commerce.

3What is new

A Piura shop owner preparing for El Niño today has four options. Each one exists and is useful; none of them gives a plan with this shop's numbers that is checked against the money the owner has.

  • CENEPRED's SIGRID (sigrid.cenepred.gob.pe/sigridv3): the national geospatial platform for disaster risk, with a map viewer, risk scenarios (including a 2025–2027 flood scenario study) and a document library. It is built for officials and planners.
  • INDECI's public preparedness guidance: the family emergency plan and the emergency backpack, for example INDECI's plan familiar de emergencia workbook and the recommendations reported by Andina. It is written for households.
  • Consultant-written contingency plans: Peru's guidelines for contingency plans are RM N° 188-2015-PCM (text; written for public entities), and Piura's Chamber of Commerce urges firms to include a contingency plan in their budget (El Tiempo). No sourced price for such a plan for a small business in Peru was found (output/tokenomics.md).
  • Asking ChatGPT directly (chatgpt.com): a general assistant that answers whatever the owner types. It was not tested in this project, so the table only describes what it offers by design.

What Pirca does differently

  • Shop-specific numbers computed in code. Stock at risk, stock to move, the emergency fund, supplier stock-up and the purchase list come from deterministic tools (on average 125 facts from 26.3 tool calls per run). The agents write {placeholders} and code fills them in.
  • Affordability check. Purchases are chosen within the preparation budget, the budget is capped at the cash on hand, and a plan whose spending exceeds the owner's cash fails.
  • Sourced citations. Every claim cites a HIST, CAT, ENFEN or BCRP ID; an unknown ID fails the plan.
  • For owners of any background. Plain Spanish (or English), a risk gauge, pictures of the shelf, the cash jar and the budget bar, a checklist, larger text, a Listen button that reads the plan aloud with the browser's Spanish voice, and a WhatsApp share button.

4The agents

Four agents work as a team. The Manager calls the three workers as tools; each worker may call only its own Python tools (backend/agents/__init__.py). Every agent uses gpt-6-luna through Portkey with low reasoning effort.

Why gpt-6-luna. It is the course model available through Portkey and it is cheap: $0.10 per 1M input tokens, $0.01 cached, $0.125 cache write and $0.50 output (Lecture 11 prices, backend/config.py). Because code does every calculation, the model only has to pick tools, read short documents and write plain text, so a small model at low effort is enough: 6 of 6 example runs passed every code check at $0.0162–$0.0201 per run.

CriticChecks the draft and tests it against hard situations; can send it back onceverify_draft, run_scenario (5 scenarios)≤ 2≤ 10

Revision loop and code checks. The Critic runs verify_draft and at least three scenarios (early rains, a bridge cut, plus a long closure, a week without power or a sick worker). If it says "revise", the Manager runs the Planner once more with the Critic's notes, then the Critic again; one revision is allowed. If verify_draft finds a problem, code forces the verdict to "revise" even if the model said "approve". Before the plan is saved, code checks it one last time: no typed numbers, only real fact keys and source IDs, no evacuation orders or own forecasts, the shop's own risk level first, the 7 sections, about 300 words, and spending within the owner's cash. Each run is also capped at 400,000 tokens, 8,000 output tokens per call and 10 model calls per agent run.

One real run, step by step: PIU-CAST26 (bodega in Castilla, Spanish)

Answers: bodega in Castilla, near the river, flooded in 2017 or 2023, ground floor, no higher storage, rented; stock S/ 20,000 with 50% below 1 m; cash S/ 3,000; fixed costs S/ 4,000/month; sales S/ 7,000/week; 2 workers; stock lasts 7 days; suppliers in Chiclayo (groceries), Piura (drinks) and Sullana (bread); preparation budget S/ 500; already has the emergency backpack.

  1. 0.0 sRun starts in Spanish. The Manager calls the Risk Analyst at 2.9 s.
  2. 3.2 sRisk Analyst: score_exposure → risk high (exposure score 100); three search_documents calls on the ENFEN communiqués and get_historical_impacts for Castilla. Done at 4.2 s.
  3. 5.7 sManager calls the Planner. At 6.9 s it runs its five tools (stock move: 3 facts, cash buffer: 8, supplier buffer: 12, action catalog, budget optimizer) and writes a draft by 24.0 s.
  4. 25.3 sCritic: verify_draft finds 0 issues; three scenarios (rains one month early, main bridge cut, power cut for a week) do not fully hold. Verdict at 35.3 s: revise.
  5. 37.7 sManager sends the Planner back with 3 revision notes (make the free steps immediate if rains come early; contact suppliers and local alternatives; rotate perishables). Revised draft at 55.7 s; the Critic checks it again from 58.2 s to 70.2 s.
  6. 76.0 sManager writes the final plan. Its verify_draft finds 7 issues (6 missing estimate asterisks, 1 too long), then 5 at 80.3 s; it fixes them.
  7. 87.5 sCode's final check: 0 issues → completed. 23 model calls, 200,515 input tokens (139,846 cached), 14,352 output, $0.0162; the automatic English translation added $0.0007.

Two real bugs found in testing, and the checks added for them

1. Unaffordable stock-up. In the first sample run (commit 8bba882) the bodega had S/ 3,000 in cash and an emergency-fund gap of S/ 692, yet the tools proposed S/ 14,000 of extra stock and the plan told the owner to stock up for 14 days. Fix (commit 908a4c4): the stock-up is capped at the cash left after the fund gap and the budget; when nothing is left the plan says "primero completa tu fondo de emergencia"; perishables get small frequent deliveries; and check_affordability fails any plan whose spending exceeds the cash (safety test 3). After the fix the same shop's stock-up is S/ 0.
2. Risk level mismatch. Plan PIU-C5TP6C showed "Medio" on the gauge but wrote "Riesgo alto": the Manager used the district's flood hazard as the shop's risk. Fix (commit 2b208f2): section 1 starts with the shop's calculated level, and verify_draft fails the plan if the first level stated does not match, in Spanish, English and translations, which triggers the Manager's fix step (safety test 6). Two example plans (PIU-FERRES, PIU-FERREN) failed the new rule and were rerun.

A bodega owner in Castilla can get, in under two minutes and in plain Spanish read aloud, a plan that combines this week's ENFEN alert, what happened on their street in 2017 and 2023, their own stock, cash and suppliers, and a purchase list that fits their budget.

8Limits and next steps

Honest limits

  • Illustrative estimates. 8 of the 15 catalog actions use an illustrative "protects" factor and 3 use an illustrative quantity (number of sacks or sheets). The plan marks them with an asterisk and a footnote, but they are placeholders, not measurements.
  • Promart web prices. Retail prices are Promart's public web catalog on 24 Oct 2026 (screens re-checked on 25 Oct: sale prices, sold out online). They exclude labor; insurance is a S/ 36/year microinsurance with a Caja Confianza loan; cloud backup is a 2023 price.
  • The Critic asks for a revision on every run (6 of 6), so the one allowed revision is always spent and its cost is always paid.
  • Data snapshots. ENFEN communiqués retrieved 24 Oct 2026 (next one due 30 Oct 2026); exchange rate 22 Oct 2026; dengue data through 2023; 15 districts. A saved plan does not update itself.
  • Read-aloud depends on the browser's voices: best in Chrome or Edge, which include Spanish voices; the button is hidden when no voice exists for the plan's language.
  • Not hosted yet. Pirca runs on one computer with a Portkey key; plans are saved as files on that machine. Hosting would add a server cost that the tokenomics above exclude.
  • No real user yet, and only six measured runs.

Next steps

  • A pilot with real owners in Castilla: time to finish the form, whether they understand the plan, and what they actually do.
  • WhatsApp access. Owners already sell through WhatsApp; let them answer the questions and receive the plan there (today the plan can only be shared to WhatsApp).
  • District rollup for a municipality. Add up the saved plans by district (stock at risk, fund gaps, supplier routes) so a municipality or the Chamber of Commerce sees where help is needed.
  • Live ENFEN updates. Load each new communiqué automatically and tell owners when their plan's alert has changed.
